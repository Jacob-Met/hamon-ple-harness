# Axial-force observability in a 2D pin-jointed truss

The `truss_2d` domain of the public `dual-verify` command returns signed axial member forces in newtons for a first-order, small-displacement pin-jointed truss. Each member has positive axial rigidity `EA`; each restrained displacement component is zero. Applied loads on restrained components contribute to support reactions. They need not extend any member. Reactions and displacements are outside this command’s returned quantities.

The calculation reports `displacement_unique` and the number of free zero-extension motions, `free_mechanism_count`. When an admissible displacement exists, every such displacement produces the same axial forces. A `compatible_mechanism` result verifies those forces and expressly identifies the undetermined motions. It provides no geometric stability, finite-displacement, buckling or capacity assessment. An applied load doing nonzero work on a free zero-extension motion has no equilibrium in this model and is refused. The separate full-field FEM verifier retains its own displacement requirements.

For example, put a pin at the left end of two unit horizontal bars, allow the right end to move horizontally on a roller, and leave the middle joint free. A horizontal 100 N pull on the right end produces 100 N tension in each bar. The middle joint’s transverse motion is free to first order. Even a small nonzero transverse load there has no first-order equilibrium. If two parallel left members have stiffnesses in a 1:3 ratio, their forces are 25 N and 75 N. Joint balance alone determines only their sum; compatibility fixes the split.

The verifier obtains the zero-extension motions from exact differences of accepted binary64 coordinates. For each raw extension row $b_e$, a positive diagonal factor $EA_e/L_e^3$ gives the stiffness contribution $EA_e b_e^T b_e/L_e^3$, with the Euclidean member length represented in binary64. Echelon elimination tests whether the free load lies in the row space and chooses independent displacement components for calculating member strain. Exact free-joint force equilibrium is checked on all components. A separate dimensionless numerical solve checks the forces before output conversion. Loss of numerical agreement or representability can withhold a result even when a formal equilibrium exists.
