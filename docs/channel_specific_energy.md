# Alternate open-channel depths at fixed specific energy

Use the registered civil/geotechnical verifier with `calculation="channel_specific_energy"`. Supply signed discharge `Q_m3_s`, wetted-section bottom width `bottom_width_m`, and positive `specific_energy_m` referenced to the local bed. The optional side slope `side_slope_z` is horizontal distance per vertical distance on each side (default zero). `gravity_m_s2` defaults to 9.80665. Rectangular, trapezoidal and triangular sections are supported. The section is prismatic, pressure is hydrostatic and the kinetic-energy correction coefficient is one.

For depth y, area A = y(b+zy), velocity V = Q/A, and specific energy E = y + V²/(2g). At fixed positive energy the available discharge squared, 2g(E−y)A², starts at zero, reaches one maximum, and returns to zero at y=E. Its intersections with the required Q² give the possible wet states. Two solutions have opposite Froude regimes and the same energy. At critical control they coincide. Below the minimum energy for the specified flow, `state="below_minimum"` and `depth_solutions=[]`. With zero discharge the sole wet state is stagnant at depth E. [U.S. Bureau of Reclamation, *Design of Gravity Dams*, appendix F](https://www.usbr.gov/tsc/techreferences/mands/mands-pdfs/GravityDams.pdf)

Each solution includes signed velocity, area, Froude number, and the independently resolved velocity head and signed Froude-squared departure from one. For example, at 4 m³/s in a 1 m wide rectangular channel with g=8 m/s² and E=2 m, the depths are 1 m (supercritical) and (1+√5)/2 m (subcritical). Their momentum functions differ. A dissipative hydraulic jump instead conserves momentum and loses specific energy; the registered `hydraulic_jump_conjugate` calculation serves that separate problem.
