# Plane-wave transmission at a fluid interface

Use the registered `compressible_acoustics` family with `subkind: "acoustics"`, densities `rho1` and `rho2` in kg/m³ and sound speeds `c1` and `c2` in m/s. The default remains normal incidence. To resolve an incident direction, supply exactly one of `incidence_angle_deg` measured from the normal pointing into medium 2, or the signed direction cosine along the interface, `incidence_sine`. Integers and binary64 direction values represent their exact submitted values. Positive and negative tilts reverse tangential phase propagation.

The model assumes two homogeneous, isotropic, inviscid lossless fluid half-spaces separated by a planar interface, with a single harmonic plane wave incident from medium 1. Tangential slowness is preserved. The transmitted normal slowness is the outward real root in the propagating regime, zero at critical incidence, and the positive imaginary root in the evanescent regime under the convention exp(i k·x − iωt). At exactly grazing incidence the incident normal power is zero, so the requested normalized coefficients are undefined. Strictly interior directions approaching grazing can be evaluated. Finite-layer tunneling, interfacial reactions and absorption are outside the model.

Pressure reflection and transmission phasors and normalized transmitted normal velocity are returned as `[real, imaginary]`. The legacy real pressure fields are populated when the phasors are real. Pressure is continuous, and both normal velocities agree at the interface. `intensity_transmission_TI` is the time-averaged transmitted **normal** power divided by incident normal power. At critical and evanescent incidence this ratio is zero, even when transmitted pressure is finite. For zero transmission `transmission_loss_dB` is null and `transmission_loss_infinite` is true.

Supplying degrees defines the angle itself. For example, at speed ratio 2 an input of exactly 30 degrees is critical. Supplying the sine defines that number itself; the two adjacent binary64 values around 0.5 belong to different regimes. Finite values must enclose a unique binary64 output and retain relative accuracy of 1e-12. The verifier replays emitted pressure and velocity phasors, the transmitted-slowness relation and normal power balance.
